### Five insights for positioning

1. **Three verdicts are not new.** Aristotle reports proved / refuted / left open; AWS has seven result types; Alloy says
   "no counterexample within the scope". What is distinctive is a REFUSED *before any model runs*, with the rule named,
   and a REFUTED whose witness anyone can recheck.
2. **Everyone with money puts a model in the translation step** (AWS, &Truth, Harmonic, Pramaana, Imandra), and each
   names it as the weak link. claimbox's statement layer is deterministic, printed back in plain words, and refusable.
3. **The closest neighbour leaves the exact ground open.** AWS returns TOO_COMPLEX on non-linear or irrational arithmetic;
   exact numeric claims about pinned files are unclaimed territory.
4. **Pinning exists in pieces** (Palomar's commit, in-toto's digests, nanopublications' hashed URIs). **No one declares how
   the bytes are read**; data-test tools inherit their engine's floating point without saying so. statcheck's rounding
   interval is the only partial precedent.
5. **Publishing the denominator is good practice, not unique** (DeepMind's formal-conjectures results, statcheck's
   coverage). What almost nobody publishes is **the refusal rate over a real corpus, with planted controls**.

### Gaps claimbox can own

- A deterministic controlled syntax for numeric, data and computation claims, with a print-back.
- The reading of the bytes as part of the claim, and forks decided per reading.
- A statement layer for crowdsourced numeric records (constants tables, leaderboards, evolutionary-search scores), where
  the theorem side is covered (formal-conjectures, Palomar) and the finite, exactly decidable side is not.
- REFUSED as a measured product number.

## 2 · The literature

*Classics' DOIs are from memory unless marked; the 2024–2026 items were checked on their arXiv, ACL, PMLR or NeurIPS pages.
The three 2026 items in §2.6 were re-checked on arXiv on 2026-10-30.*

### 2.1 Architecture and trust
- Gordon, Milner, Wadsworth 1979, *Edinburgh LCF*, LNCS 78 — only a small kernel makes theorems. **Supports** "claimbox never decides".
- Barendregt & Wiedijk 2005, "The challenge of computer mathematics", Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 363 — the de Bruijn criterion.
  **Supports** the small separate checker; says nothing about the translator.
- Necula 1997, "Proof-carrying code", POPL, doi:10.1145/263699.263712 — the producer ships a certificate the consumer checks cheaply.
- Pnueli, Siegel, Singerman 1998, "Translation validation", TACAS, doi:10.1007/BFb0054170; Necula 2000, PLDI — validate each
  run instead of verifying the translator. **Supports** step 6's method and **limits** it: translation validation needs a
  formal source, and prose has none, so step 6 validates stand-ins (anchors, print-back, plants), never equivalence.
- McConnell, Mehlhorn, Näher, Schweitzer 2011, "Certifying algorithms", Comput. Sci. Rev. 5(2), doi:10.1016/j.cosrev.2010.09.009
  — every output carries a witness. **Suggests** CERTIFIED should carry one too.
- Avizienis 1985, N-version programming, IEEE TSE 11(12); **Knight & Leveson 1986**, IEEE TSE 12(1) — independently written
  versions fail together far more often than independence predicts. **Contradicts** counting agreement as independent.
- **Kim, Garg, Peng, Garg 2025**, "Correlated errors in large language models", ICML, arXiv:2506.07962 — when two models are
  both wrong they usually agree on the wrong answer; larger models correlate more, across providers. **Constrains** step 6.
- **Goel et al. 2025**, "Great models think alike …", ICML, arXiv:2502.04313 — error similarity rises with capability; model
  judges favour models like themselves. **Constrains** step 6.

### 2.2 Controlled natural languages
- Kuhn 2014, "A survey and classification of controlled natural languages", Comput. Linguist. 40(1) — places claimbox at
  high precision, low expressiveness.
- Fuchs, Kaljurand, Kuhn 2008, "Attempto Controlled English for knowledge representation" — deterministic parsing with a
  paraphrase back to the writer. **Supports** the print-back.
- Ranta 2004, "Grammatical Framework", J. Funct. Program. 14(2) — one abstract syntax, reversible grammars. **The print-back
  should be the grammar run backwards, not a separate template.**
- Cramer et al. 2009, "The Naproche project", CNL 2009 — a mathematical controlled language checked by provers.
- Kowalski et al. 2023, "Logical English for law and education" (DOI unverified) — templates declared once.
- Merigoux, Chataing, Protzenko 2021, "Catala", PACMPL 5(ICFP) — code laid out against the statute. **Supports** step 8.

### 2.3 Ambiguity
- Bos 1996, "Predicate logic unplugged" (Hole Semantics); Copestake, Flickinger, Pollard, Sag 2005, "Minimal Recursion
  Semantics" — one underspecified form, the readings enumerated. **Supports** forks and holes.
- van Fraassen 1966, supervaluation — a sentence is true iff true under every precisification. **The semantics of a fork.**
- Liu et al. 2023, *AmbiEnt* (EMNLP); Saparina & Lapata 2024, *AMBROSIA* (NeurIPS D&B) — strong models fail to detect
  and cover readings. **The readings must come from the library, not from the proposer.**

### 2.4 Logics of verdicts
- Kleene 1952, strong three-valued connectives — how words compose in compound claims.
- Belnap 1977, "A useful four-valued logic"; Ginsberg 1988, bilattices — "neither" and "both" beside true and false. **A
  fork can come out split, which is not REFUSED.**
- Libkin 2016, "SQL's three-valued logic and certain answers", ACM TODS — a warning on how REFUSED propagates.
- Green, Karvounarakis, Tannen 2007, "Provenance semirings", PODS, doi:10.1145/1265530.1265535 — what a verdict rests on.

### 2.5 Exact numerics and reading bytes
- Moore 1966 / Moore, Kearfott, Cloud 2009, interval analysis; Johansson 2017, Arb.
- Weihrauch 2000, computable analysis; Richardson 1968 — equality of reals, and zero-equivalence of elementary expressions,
  are undecidable. **A REFUSED on a real identity is principled.**
- Goldberg 1991, "What every computer scientist should know about floating-point arithmetic", ACM CSUR 23(1),
  doi:10.1145/103162.103163; Clinger 1990 and Steele & White 1990 (PLDI) — decimal↔binary conversion has conventions.
- RFC 8259 §6 (2017) — JSON numbers have no specified precision. **The JSON standard itself admits both readings of the 605
  case.**
- Xin 2026, "Where the verifier fails", arXiv:2609.01354 — a metamorphic audit of math verifiers; tolerance checks accept
  off-by-one answers at large magnitudes.

### 2.6 Autoformalization and faithfulness — the three that change the design
1. **Mei & Douglas 2026, "Beyond LLM Judge: Mechanical Verification for Agentic Autoformalization", arXiv:2610.20573**
   (v1 14 Oct, v2 21 Oct 2026). Numerical checks against the source's own numbers and vacuity checks, beside model review,
   reach "the lowest accepted-error rate among the evaluated methods" (abstract). Its tables (from the first pass, not
   re-read): a numerical check caught a false bound that back-translation and every judge accepted; the price is a high
   share of correct items flagged. **→ Mechanical checks beat judges; expect and publish a high refusal rate.**
2. **Zhang et al. 2026, "Beyond Compilation: Evaluating Faithful Natural-Language-to-Lean Statement Formalization",
   arXiv:2606.31002** (v4, 15 Oct 2026). On 227 statements and eight systems, compiling overstates faithfulness by 1.3 to
   29.5 points; a tool-using agent compiles 87.2% and 57.7% are accepted; Lean feedback drives most of the compilation gain
   and nearly half of that gain fails the semantic criterion; a two-judge criterion agrees with the human majority on 91.5%.
   **→ Checker feedback improves well-formedness, not faithfulness: "admitted" is never shown as "faithful".**
3. **Ammanamanchi, Bhat, Biderman 2026, "Faults in Our Formal Benchmarking", arXiv:2606.29493** (ICML 2026 per the arXiv
   comments). Static checkers over five Lean benchmarks surface 4,833 findings, 398 mechanically certified (counterexamples,
   vacuous theorems, unsound axioms); defects inflate and deflate scores. **→ The library states its conventions
   (totalised subtraction, x/0); the calibration corpus needs its own audit.**

Also: Wu et al. 2022 (NeurIPS, arXiv:2205.12615); miniF2F (ICLR 2022); ProofNet (2023); FormalAlign (ICLR 2025,
arXiv:2410.10135 — a learned alignment score; later measured to accept many wrong statements, so not a gate); BEq (ICLR
2025) and Poiroux et al. 2025 (EMNLP) — equivalence decided symbolically; nl2postcond (FSE 2024) and Clover (SAIV 2024) —
specifications scored by mutants and by consistency.

### 2.7 Testing the translation
- Chen, Cheung, Yiu 1998, metamorphic testing; Segura et al. 2016 survey (IEEE TSE 42(9)); Ribeiro et al. 2020, CheckList
  (ACL); Jia & Harman 2011, mutation analysis. **Step 6's plants are metamorphic relations**: change a source digit and the
  object moves the same way; paraphrase the claim and the object does not move.

### 2.8 Claim verification in language processing
- FEVER (Thorne et al. 2018); Schuster et al. 2019 — claim-only models score well from wording artifacts. **A verdict must
  depend on the bytes: plant the source, keep the claim fixed.**
- SciFact (2020); AVeriTeC (2023) — "not enough evidence" and "conflicting" labels. QuanTemp (SIGIR 2024) — numerical
  claims are where language-processing verification is weakest.

### 2.9 Scientific claims as data
- Groth, Gibson, Velterop 2010, nanopublications; ORKG (K-CAP 2019).
- **Nuijten et al. 2016, statcheck** (Behav. Res. Methods 48) and **Brown & Heathers 2017, GRIM** (SPPS 8(4)) — recomputing
  printed statistics. **The closest precedents for steps 6 and 8.**
- Massot, leanblueprint — document nodes linked to Lean declarations and marked by status. **The precedent for step 8.**

### 2.10 Search with verifiers
- Cobbe et al. 2021 (verifiers); AlphaCode (Science 2022); FunSearch (Nature 2024); AlphaEvolve (arXiv:2506.13131);
  Snell et al. 2024; Brown et al. 2024 ("Large Language Monkeys"); Chen et al. 2021 (pass@k).
- **Stroebl, Kapoor, Narayanan 2024, arXiv:2411.17501** — resampling is capped by the verifier's false-positive rate.
- **Georgiev, Gómez-Serrano, Tao, Wagner 2025, arXiv:2511.02864** — the search found exploits in scoring code; the fix was
  exact or interval arithmetic with conservative bounds.
- Reward hacking against graders: ImpossibleBench (arXiv:2510.20270), MacDiarmid et al. 2025 (arXiv:2511.18397), Helff et
  al. 2026 (arXiv:2604.15149), Ray 2026 (arXiv:2606.01066). **The loop will find the loophole.**

### 2.11 "For all" claims and machines
- Rado 1962; Aaronson 2020, "The Busy Beaver Frontier" (SIGACT News) — the Π₁ framing; Yedidia & Aaronson 2016.
- bbchallenge 2025, arXiv:2509.12337 — S(5) = 47,176,870 proved in Coq; the wiki's "Cryptid" entry.
- Dos Santos et al. 2026, "At the Limits of Computability: Steps Towards Autonomous AI Discovery on the Busy Beaver
  Frontier", arXiv:2610.22603; github.com/google-deepmind/cryptids (the label "subject to community acceptance").
- Oliveira e Silva, Herzog, Pardi 2014 (Math. Comp.) — Goldbach checked to 4·10¹⁸: the model of a "searched to N" report;
  Odlyzko & te Riele 1985 — Mertens disproved far beyond any search.

## 3 · Five findings that change the design

1. **Agreement is not independence (step 6).** Count two proposals as two only when they differ in kind (the pattern
   reader, a person, a model; two models from different families at the least). Gate on the plants — the source mutated,
   and both proposals must move — not on agreement over the clean source. Publish each pair's measured same-error rate.
2. **Mechanical checks, a deterministic print-back (step 6).** The print-back is the grammar run backwards over the
   object, never a model. Add a vacuity check (the word does not change when the bytes are planted → the claim does not
   depend on them) and a sensitivity check (move each anchored number by one unit of its last printed digit; the word or
   the witness must react where the arithmetic says it should). Publish the flag rate.
3. **The library supplies the readings; a fork reports every reading (steps 4–5).** Each definition declares its
   conventions (printed digits or binary64, totalised subtraction, x/0). Forks are generated from those declarations.
   claimbox shows every reading's word; when they differ it says *split* — a description, not a verdict.
4. **The loop will find the loophole (step 10).** The target object is fixed and pinned before sampling, so proposers
   cannot choose the reading or a weaker claim; red controls run against the checker before each loop; degenerate
   witnesses are rejected; the denominators are published, including the filter's own plant recall.
5. **A "for all" claim is never CERTIFIED by search (step 7).** A watcher reports *not refuted up to N* (pin, date); a
   document shows N and never counts it as certified. "There exists" claims are the mirror case.

## 4 · Claims claimbox must not make

- That it establishes faithfulness to the author's intent. It does not; the best methods of 2026 still accept some wrong
  statements.
- That two models agreeing is independent confirmation.
- That CERTIFIED means the source sentence is true. It means *this object, under this reading, holds on these pinned bytes*.
- That "searched to N" is evidence for the universal claim.
- That real-number identities are decided in general (Richardson). Only forms the checker admits are decided.
- That a small checker cannot be gamed. The de Bruijn criterion shrinks what must be trusted; it does not remove it.
- That models can list a claim's readings.
- That the cryptids are solved, or that "Cryptid" status is settled.
- Novelty of the parts. statcheck, GRIM, Attempto, Clover and nanopublications came first; what is defensible is the
  combination — deterministic, anchored, pinned, exact, three verdicts, aware of forks, refusal rate published.
