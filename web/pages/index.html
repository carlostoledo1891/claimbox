claimbox · the claim language for cert-machine

Claims are written in words no machine can check. claimbox states them so one can.

claimbox turns a sentence into an exact claim: the bytes it is about, how they are read, and each bound as the exact number its digits mean. cert-machine then decides it with one of three words, CERTIFIED, REFUTED or REFUSED. When a sentence cannot be stated exactly, claimbox says why instead of guessing.

proof 1 of 3 · honesty

It says why when it cannot

{{cov_ref}} of {{cov_ref}}
refusals in cert-machine’s register that name their reason

Most claims fail to be checkable for an ordinary reason: the bytes they are about were never published, the sentence prints no number, or it states a limit no finite run can reach. claimbox names the reason for each one, out of {{cov_n}} sentences. A sentence is never silently dropped and never guessed at.

proof 2 of 3 · proposals

A model writes the claim. Checks decide whether it counts.

{{pr_mf}} of {{pr_m}}
claims a model wrote that equal the decided answer

Given {{pr_n}} AI-written sentences one at a time, a model wrote the claim each one makes. None of the checks is a model. Every number must be found in its sentence, and the claim must read back with the sentence’s own relation. It must move exactly when one digit of the sentence’s bar is moved, and stay put when the sentence is reworded. When the model slipped on a reworded sentence, the claim was refused ({{pa_moved}}).

A sentence counts as validated only when a second, different kind of proposer agrees. Today that is {{pr_valid}}, and the next step is a person.

Two ways to use it

for anyone who publishes a number

Say exactly what you claim

Write the claim once in claimbox: the file, its reading, the bound. claimbox prints it back in plain words from the object cert-machine decides, never from what was typed. A reader then knows exactly what was checked, and on which bytes.

for teams whose models write claims

Count only what holds up

Let a model propose, and let claimbox validate. Each number must be found in the source and the claim must read back with the sentence’s relation. The bar is moved and the claim must move with it; the sentence is reworded and the claim must stay put. A second kind of proposer must agree. What fails is named, not hidden.

how it works

From a sentence to one of three words

  1. The sentenceas the author printed it, in a file anyone can open.{{edge_claim}}
  2. The claimwritten in claimbox’s language: which bytes, how they are read, which bound. If that cannot be done exactly, the reason is named.
  3. The objectthe pinned bytes, their reading, and each bound as the exact number its digits mean, printed back in plain words.
  4. cert-machine’s wordfrom a separate program that shares no code with claimbox: CERTIFIED, REFUTED with the object that breaks it, or REFUSED with the reason.{{edge_word}}

Small parts, each checkable

cert-machineThe only judge. claimbox never prints a verdict of its own; it shows cert-machine’s.
The languageClaims, holes and forks: every reading of the bytes written down, every reading decided.
The libraryEach definition written once and used by name, from step functions to the busy beavers.
The grammar of evidenceSolid for decided, dashed for computed, dotted for chosen. The red × only marks a witness.

  1. The sentenceand where it is printed: a paper, a README, a post, a model’s output.
  2. The bytesit is about, or where they live. A claim about data no one can open stays a named hole.
