claimbox · an experiment

xx = y, by fractions

An AI proposes the numbers, claimbox states them as claims, cert-machine decides them.

It began with one picture, xx = 64. The equation has one real root, and no fraction equals it. What a fraction can do is trap it: two fractions on either side, each side one comparison of two whole numbers, decided exactly. This page does that for every y to a thousand, and reads what the fractions show.

what the fractions show

Four things the continued fractions say

Read from the computed roots. The brackets that decide them are cert-machine’s; the patterns are observations, and they are labelled so.

One: only kk has a fraction for a root

The root is a whole number exactly at y = {{ex0}}, {{ex1}}, {{ex2}} and {{ex3}}, and cert-machine pins each one from both sides: x ≥ k and x ≤ k, both certified. For every other whole y the root is not even algebraic. If it were a fraction p/q that is not whole, xx would be irrational; if it were an irrational algebraic number, xx would be transcendental by the Gelfond–Schneider theorem. A theorem cited, not decided here. So {{n_exact}} roots are fractions, and every other one is a number no fraction will ever equal.

Two: near kk, one enormous step

Just off kk the root sits just off k, by about 1/(kk(1 + ln k)). Its continued fraction says so with one huge quotient: at y = {{y255}} it is {{n255}}, where the slope predicts {{p255}}; at y = {{y3126}} it is {{n3126}}, against {{p3126}}. The closest fraction there is the whole number k, and it is right to three or four decimals.

Three: elsewhere, the roots look like typical numbers

Pool the partial quotients of all the irrational roots and they follow the law that almost every real number obeys: a quotient of 1 occurs {{gk1}} of the time, against {{gk1l}}. Their geometric mean is {{geo}}, against Khinchin’s constant {{khin}}, and (ln qn)/n averages {{levy_o}}, against Lévy’s {{levy}}. The roots of xx = y keep no secret pattern in their digits. These are observations on {{n_roots}} computed roots, not a proof.

Four: a few roots have fractions that are almost too good

A large partial quotient makes the convergent just before it unusually close. The largest found is {{big_a}}, in the root for y = {{big_y}}. Its convergent sits closer to the root than any fraction of its size has a right to. For y = {{y100}}, the fraction {{c100}} is already off by only {{e100}}.

how it was done

Proposed, stated, decided

  1. The AI proposeseach root by Newton’s method, its continued fraction, and the fractions that bracket it (tools/xx_study.py). Computed, not trusted.
  2. claimbox stateseach bracket as a claim in its language, xroot(n) > p/q. Its library turns the claim into one exact comparison of whole numbers, and reads y from a pinned file.
  3. cert-machine decideseach comparison from the object alone, as a separate program: CERTIFIED, or REFUTED for the planted wrong brackets.
decided
exact arithmetic by cert-machine, or a count from a record
computed
a number the AI computed; nothing decided it
refused
no mark: ∅ and a word
why these numbers stand as they do
  • A root’s digits are computed. The fractions trapping it are decided, each by one exact comparison.
  • Uniqueness of the root rests on an argument written into the claim: xx ≤ 1 on (0, 1], increasing above 1, and no negative root. cert-machine does not decide that argument.
  • Every number in the text is read from a record, and the page’s build reads it again.
